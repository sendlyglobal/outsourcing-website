## DELIVERY METHODOLOGY / PROCESS PAGE (Timeline)

**Section intro paragraph**
Every project follows the same seven-phase process, whether it's a two-month mobile app or a year-long ERP rollout. The phases don't change — only their length does. Here's exactly what happens at each stage, and what you can expect to see by the end of it.

**Step 1 — Discovery & Requirements** *(typically 1–2 weeks)*
We start with structured workshops involving the people who'll actually use the system, not just the stakeholders who commissioned it. The goal is to map current workflows, surface pain points, and agree on what success actually looks like before any scoping happens. You walk away with a requirements document and a project brief that both sides sign off on — so there's a shared definition of "done" from day one.

**Step 2 — Solution Architecture & Planning** *(typically 1–2 weeks)*
Our architects translate the requirements into a concrete technical approach: system architecture, integrations, data model, and tech stack. This becomes a sprint roadmap with milestones and a fixed or capped estimate, so you know the shape of the project — and its cost — before development begins.

**Step 3 — UI/UX Design** *(typically 2–3 weeks, often overlapping with late architecture work)*
We build wireframes first to nail down structure and flow, then move into high-fidelity mockups and a clickable prototype. You review and sign off at both stages, so development starts from a design that's already been validated — not one we're guessing will work.

**Step 4 — Development (Agile Sprints)** *(duration varies by scope — the longest phase)*
Engineering runs in 1–2 week sprints, each ending in a working build you can actually see and test. A shared project board and regular sprint demos mean you're never waiting weeks for a status update — you can watch the product take shape in real time.

**Step 5 — QA & Testing** *(ongoing through development, plus 1–2 weeks dedicated pre-launch)*
Testing isn't a phase we bolt on at the end — unit tests, integration tests, and manual QA run against every sprint build. Before launch, we run a dedicated hardening pass covering performance, security, and cross-device compatibility, so nothing that reaches production is untested.

**Step 6 — Deployment & Launch** *(typically 3–5 days)*
We deploy in stages: first to a staging environment for final verification, then to production through a controlled release — often a soft launch or phased rollout for higher-risk systems. You get a rollback plan and full deployment documentation, not just a live link and a shrug.

**Step 7 — Post-Launch Support & Handoff** *(2–4 weeks hypercare, then ongoing as agreed)*
The weeks right after launch are when real-world usage surfaces issues staging never could, so we stay closely engaged through a defined hypercare period. After that, you can move to an ongoing support arrangement or take full ownership — either way, you get complete documentation and codebase access, not a system only we can maintain.

**Closing line for the process page**
This process is deliberately the same for every client, because predictability is the whole point of outsourcing to a partner you can trust — not something we improvise per project.
